# Integrated literature notes from the ICSOR article

The reference inventory and JSON contain all 71 bibliography entries from the published component article. The conceptual notes below support a cohesive literature review. They distinguish proposed methods and physical scope without transferring the component article's empirical findings into the dissertation.

## Treatment objectives and process interpretation

Lin2022 and Madhav2019 connect water pollution to environmental and health consequences. Their role is to motivate reliable treatment rather than to justify a particular surrogate. Duan2022 describes biological nitrogen-removal pathways. Henze1999 and Henze2006 supply the component bookkeeping and coupled activated sludge transformations. Wentzel1992 joins carbon, nitrogen, and excess phosphorus removal in one process explanation. Guerrero2011 explains why carbon source affects competition between phosphorus-storing organisms and denitrifiers. This sequence supports an educational transition from treatment goals to the need for component predictions.

StenstromSong1991 explains how oxygen transport and competition affect nitrification. Its primary author bibliography at https://seas.ucla.edu/stenstro/referreed.html confirms Research Journal of the Water Pollution Control Federation 63(3), 208–219, 1991, although that listing describes “mass transport” rather than the “oxygen transport” wording in the component article. It belongs in the process discussion of why an aeration effect depends on substrate loading and microbial state. Yilmaz2007 addresses biomass activity under alternating conditions and starvation. In the adopted methods it supports physically informed initial guesses, while the dissertation should avoid claiming that such guesses certify biological viability or uniqueness of the nonlinear steady state.

Araujo2013 links operating sensitivity with economic controlled-variable selection. Machado2014 distinguishes influent uncertainty and operational uncertainty from identifiable model parameters. Together they explain why operating variables and influent loads are separated in the explicit regression library. They also qualify the interpretation of coefficient signs. A fitted coefficient can depend on sampling and parameter tradeoffs even when the model is inspectable.

## From approximate process models to learned surrogates

BhosekarIerapetritou2018 reviews surrogate construction, feasibility analysis, and optimization. Approximation accuracy and feasible engineering use are connected but different requirements. Durkin2024 brings surrogate optimization to wastewater resource recovery. Tsochatzidi2025 supplies a broader process-system example from pharmaceutical manufacturing and should be identified as an adjacent domain rather than wastewater evidence. Aboagye2021, PadronPaez2020, and Tang2026 connect wastewater network choices with optimization and sustainability. These sources support the motivation for repeated low-cost evaluations without suggesting that any accurate regression automatically supplies a valid optimization landscape.

Ahn2014, Kim2009, and Lim2011 provide operating-factor and response-surface approaches that connect fitted response relations to wastewater operating decisions. Nazlabadi2021 gives a systematic historical view of response surface methodology in biological wastewater treatment. This group makes the move to a transparent second-order library intelligible. Polynomial terms are not arbitrary feature engineering. They retain a familiar approximation structure while permitting curvature and operation-loading interactions. Their finite order also sets a clear expressiveness limit.

Li2020 and Yang2014 describe fuzzy or regime-based dissolved-oxygen control approximations. Explain why local or regime-specific approximations can make nonlinear processes tractable and why their reliability depends on the operating domain. These sources should not be summarized as generic global linearizations without examining their actual fuzzy model structure.

Wold2001 provides the foundations of partial least squares. Khatri2020 surveys its use for water-quality analysis. Woo2009 uses kernel partial least squares for industrial cokes wastewater process estimation. Liu2021 uses a dynamic concurrent kernel treatment for monitoring. Yang2021 embeds a relevance-vector inner model in partial least squares for adaptive effluent prediction. A useful synthesis distinguishes transparent latent linear relations from nonlinear extensions. The discussion should explain what extension supplies the nonlinear representation rather than asserting that every partial least squares method is simply linear or inadequate.

Asadi2017 connects data mining with aeration optimization. Bagherzadeh2021 connects feature selection and nitrogen prediction. SadriMoghaddamMesghali2023 describes a hybrid ensemble for effluent nitrogen prediction at a combined trickling-filter and activated sludge facility. Mao2024 relates artificial intelligence to effluent quality and aeration energy. Yang2025 extends the predicted quantities to waste activated sludge generation and operating efficiency. Newhart2019 gives plant-level context for these data-driven analyses. Wang2025 compares learned interpretable models with a mechanistic nitrogen predictor. Xiao2026 joins mechanistic dynamics with an interpretable surrogate. These sources establish the range of prediction and decision tasks. They should be discussed by their task and information requirements rather than introduced as a list of algorithm names.

BaAlawi2021 concerns sensor validation through stacked denoising autoencoders. Its strongest role is input quality, measurement reliability, and the distinction between a trustworthy input and a trustworthy surrogate prediction. It is not the central foundational citation for leakage control. Pinheiro2025 addresses scaling. Use it to explain that numerical magnitudes and preprocessing affect regression and interpretation. Training-only preprocessing is an explicit methodological rule of the dissertation and need not be attributed as if established uniquely by either study.

## Conservation methods and the inequality gap

SturmWexler2020 introduces conservation-aware surrogate prediction using reaction structure and null-space reasoning. SturmWexler2022 imposes conservation by architecture through a flux-to-tendency transformation. They support the distinction between predicting an arbitrary component state and learning quantities that are transformed through stoichiometry. The dissertation stoichiometric matrix has reactions in rows and components in columns. Its conserved vectors occupy the right null space, so A=V0 transpose and A nu transpose=0. The component article's phrase “left null space of nu” is dimensionally wrong and must not be repeated.

Beucler2021 examines analytic physical constraints in neural emulation and supports explicit equality-enforcement or completion architectures. Geiss2022 addresses physically consistent atmospheric chemical downscaling. Harder2022 addresses physics-informed aerosol microphysics. Hansen2024 examines learning physical models that respect conservation laws and provides a reconciliation or probabilistic perspective. Ruckstuhl2021 studies mass-conserving neural assimilation. Raissi2019 supplies a foundational example of training with physical residuals as penalties. Synthesize these contributions by how the constraint enters the prediction, including architecture, completion, reconciliation, and training penalties. A soft loss term is not a deployment equality, and a conservation equality is not by itself a concentration lower bound.

SturmSilva2024 is the existing key for the published 2025 paper with DOI 10.1021/acsestair.4c00220. It develops a nearest conservative correction and species-aware weighting. Explain why scale and uncertainty matter when a small concentration can be overwhelmed by changes that look small in aggregate. The dissertation's unweighted affine projection has a Euclidean distance in the adopted numerical coordinates. Its later weighted absolute correction is a different objective, not the same algorithm as the atmospheric species-weighted projection.

KircherVotsmeier2025 explicitly brings atom balance and positivity together for mechanistic kinetics surrogates. It explains why an equality-only correction can move a small component below zero, and why simply clipping afterward can break conservation. Its scaled-space positivity strategy motivates the need for simultaneous constraints. The dissertation uses a linear program around the affine conservative reference and must describe that methodological difference accurately.

Sturm2023 uses mass-conserving superspecies transport and provides another example of conservation with nonnegative composition. Its atmospheric transport state and scaling procedure differ from a steady-state activated sludge effluent correction. This source should appear in the comparison of joint physical requirements rather than be used to imply a wastewater precedent.

Min2024 addresses hard-constrained neural networks and approximation capacity with input-dependent affine and convex constraints. Primary identifiers are arXiv 2410.10807 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10807. The originally cited record lists Min, Sonar, and Azizan. A current author-hosted PDF at https://azizan.mit.edu/papers/HardNet.pdf lists Min and Azizan. Preserve source-specific authors and dates consistently. The method contributes a distinction between enforcing constraints only after learning and carrying an enforcement layer through learning. It does not prove universal approximation for the particular finite second-order dissertation regression.

Utkarsh2025 addresses a differentiable probabilistic projection layer that uses variance information to impose hard constraints and quantify uncertainty. Primary identifiers are arXiv 2506.07003 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07003. A published 2026 conference counterpart is available at https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/17bae482066287ce54814514cf12f248-Abstract-Conference.html. The dissertation regression is deterministic and its correction weights are fixed. Explain the probabilistic method as related general constraint learning rather than assign its uncertainty capabilities to ICSOR.

## Interpretability and coupled estimation

Rudin2019 supports the preference for transparent model structure when decisions require scrutiny. Shen2023 connects learned and physical modeling in geosciences and supports careful differentiation of the complete mapping. Brunton2016 uses an explicit nonlinear library to identify governing structure. These references support an educational distinction between having named coefficients and recovering a unique causal mechanism. ICSOR has explicit blocks but its driver and coupling matrix can trade off. Coefficient symmetry resolves duplicate ordered quadratic terms without proving joint identifiability.

Razaviyayn2013 supplies conditions for convergence of block successive minimization. Each exact convex block update is non-increasing when the previous value remains admissible. The coupling condition-number screen is a separate nonlinear safeguard. Shrinking a convex-program proposal can increase the objective, so the dissertation needs an acceptance check or a narrower convergence claim. A bounded monotone objective converges in value. This alone is neither a global optimum nor an unconditional stationary-point guarantee.

Stellato2018 is the precise conference reference in the component article with DOI 10.1109/CONTROL.2018.8516834. It concerns an operator-splitting quadratic-program solver. HuangfuHall2018 concerns dual revised simplex methods and has DOI 10.1007/s12532-017-0130-5. Discuss the mathematical problem class each solver supports. Do not present algorithm names as evidence of physical guarantees. The constraints define the guarantee and verified numerical solutions realize it.

## Evaluation as a separate methodological layer

McKay1979 supports Latin hypercube sampling. CurtissHirschfelder1952 supports stiff integration as the foundation for dynamic relaxation. Akiba2019 supplies automated tuning, while Bergstra2011 supplies the tree-structured Parzen estimator. Their roles belong in the shared methods rather than detailed introduction prose.

Geman1992 explains the bias-variance issue behind comparing model flexibility as sample size changes. VieringLoog2023 reviews learning curves and explains why one training size cannot establish data efficiency. Cawley2011 and Guyon2011 give active-learning precedents for evaluating trajectories and area summaries. The dissertation repeated random-size design differs from active selection of labels. It borrows a curve-summary idea without claiming an active-learning experiment.

Demsar2006 gives average-rank and statistical-comparison foundations for multiple datasets. Shevchenko2024 stresses variability and stable benchmarking in recommendation systems. Their domain differences should be explicit. A dense rank averaged across targets, metrics, and sample sizes is descriptive. It does not become a significance test by citing Demsar. Repeated partitions reveal sensitivity to sampling, while fixed tuning settings prevent retuning from being mixed with sample-size effects.

The planned comparison should preserve stage identity. Raw-head physical diagnostics assess learned behavior. Equality-aligned comparator accuracy assesses a corrected evaluation quantity and must be labeled as such. Final ICSOR accuracy assesses checked deployment. Correction magnitude and fitting/deployment time answer additional questions. None of these endpoints should be inferred from the others.
